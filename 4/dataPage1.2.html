Main Sequence Star: Orange Dwarf

IMAGE: Alpha Centarui B (to the right) | IMAGE CREDIT: NASA/ESA

Dusty's Log Entry:

  • Type: K-class Main Sequence Star
  • Temperature: about 3,900-5,300 K
  • Mass: about 0.45 - 0.8 Suns
  • Lifespan: 15-45 billion years
  • What's Next: evolving into a red giant, then a white dwarf

Orange dwarfs are slightly cooler than the Sun and shine steady for longer times. They're considered better for advanced life, given that the Sun will grow and dry the Earth until it's uninhabitable. An orange dwarf is the perfect star as it's habital zone is much longer than the sun. This habital zone means the star's conditions are just right, it's neither too hot nor too cold for liquid water to exist on a planet's surface. Orange dwarfs are referred to as "Goldilock stars" for hosting life.

Example of the orange dwarf includes Alpha Centauri B.
