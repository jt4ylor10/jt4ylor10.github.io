Rare and Extreme Stars: Yellow Hypergiant

IMAGE: Rho Cassiopeiae in Optical Light | IMAGE CREDIT: David Ritter

Dusty's Log Entry:

  • Type: hypergiant
  • Temperature: 4,000 - 8,000 K
  • Mass: a few dozen Suns
  • Lifespan: very short
  • What's Next: likely a supernova

Yellow hypergiants are amongst the rarest of stars, with only a handful actually being identified. These stars are enormous, being hundreds of times wider than the Sun and hundreds of thousands times brighter. They sit in an unstable stage between red and blue supergiants. The outer layers of a yellow supergiant are barely held together, causing them to pulse and throw off outbursts of gas. These outbursts of material form huge dusty envelopes and extend the atmosphere around the star.

Examples of yellow hypergiants include Rho Cassiopeiae and HR 5171.
